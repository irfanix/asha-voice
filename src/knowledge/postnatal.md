# Postnatal care for the mother — quick reference for ASHA Didi

Source: Home Based Newborn Care (HBNC) guidelines and National Health Mission (NHM) ASHA guidelines. Verify against the current documents before printing or distribution.

## Danger signs in the mother after delivery (refer TODAY)

- Heavy bleeding after delivery (postpartum haemorrhage)
- High fever
- Foul-smelling discharge
- Convulsions / fits
- Severe abdominal pain
- Difficulty breathing

Heavy bleeding or fits after delivery is a life-threatening emergency. Call 108 and refer to the nearest hospital immediately.

## Watch signs (follow up within 1 to 3 days)

- Breast pain, redness or a lump — may be early mastitis
- Mild fever, mild pain in the lower belly
- Baby blues or low mood

If any of these get worse, treat them as danger signs and refer the mother the same day.

## Routine postnatal care (counselling the mother)

- Rest as much as possible for the first six weeks. Ask family for help with chores.
- Eat one extra meal a day. Drink plenty of water, especially if breastfeeding.
- Continue iron and folic acid tablets for at least 180 days after delivery.
- Continue calcium if it was prescribed during pregnancy.
- Breastfeed exclusively for the first six months. Feed on demand, day and night.
- Keep the perineum clean and dry. Change pads frequently.
- Use a modern contraceptive method if the family wants to delay the next pregnancy. Discuss options with the ANM or PHC.
- Attend the postnatal check-up at the PHC around day 3 to 7, and again at day 42.

## Postnatal visit windows (aligned with HBNC)

- Day 1 (home delivery): first postnatal check for mother and baby
- Day 3: mother and baby check
- Day 7: mother and baby check
- Day 14, 21 and 28: mother and baby check
- Day 42: final postnatal visit and family planning counselling

## Family planning methods to mention

- Exclusive breastfeeding (LAM) in the first six months, if the mother meets the criteria
- Condoms
- Oral contraceptive pills
- Injectable contraceptive (Antara)
- Intrauterine device (IUD / CuT)
- Female sterilisation (for those who have completed their family)

The ASHA can inform and refer. The actual choice and prescription is the doctor's or ANM's job.

## What to tell the family

- Mother should rest and eat well.
- Keep the baby warm and breastfeed often.
- Come to the PHC immediately if the mother or baby shows any danger sign.
- Use a family planning method if the next pregnancy should be delayed.

## What ASHA Voice will not do

- It will not diagnose any illness in the mother.
- It will not prescribe or dose any medicine.
- It will not recommend a specific family planning method — that is a decision made with the PHC.

If in doubt, refer to the PHC.
